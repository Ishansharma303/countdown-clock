# Countdown Clock Kickstarter Campaign Package

Status: Draft campaign strategy and production-readiness checklist  
Campaign category: Design / Technology, subject to Kickstarter review  
Audience: Adults 18+  
Working name: Countdown Clock; complete trademark, domain, and marketplace checks before naming the campaign

## Campaign strategy

### Core idea

A normal clock tells you what time it is. Countdown Clock helps you decide what the time means.

The campaign should lead with the physical object's everyday modes—time lived, milestones, goals, habits, and finite moments. The optional lifespan range is the provocative differentiator, but it must not become an unsupported promise that the product predicts death.

### Primary message

**Make your time visible.**

### Draft title

**Countdown Clock — Make Your Time Visible**

### Draft subtitle

An always-on desk clock for the seconds you have lived, the goals ahead, and an optional illustrative life-planning horizon.

### Thirty-second pitch

> Time is our most limited resource, but most clocks only help us schedule the next meeting. Countdown Clock is a calm, always-on desk object that can show every second you have lived, count down to a meaningful goal, track time since you began something important, or display an optional population-based planning range. It works without a subscription, keeps health answers local by default, and lets you choose the reminder that helps you act today.

### Category language

Call the product:

- Adult motivational desk clock
- Reflective time display
- Connected clock with local setup
- General-wellness product

Do not call it:

- Death clock
- Mortality predictor
- Medical device
- Accurate age-at-death calculator
- Toy for children

## Audience and use cases

### Primary backer profiles

1. **Intentional planner:** Uses journals, goals, and yearly reviews; wants long horizons made concrete.
2. **Maker or quantified-self buyer:** Likes transparent data products and wants to understand the model and local processing.
3. **Creative professional:** Wants an always-visible project or launch countdown without another phone notification.
4. **Meaningful gift buyer:** Wants a configurable object, but the recipient must privately choose any health-related setup.

### Campaign use cases

- “I have been alive for 1,234,567,890 seconds.”
- “There are 62 days until I ship my first book.”
- “I have practiced for 400 days.”
- “I may have about 1,900 weekends in this illustrative range—how will I use the next one?”
- “My ordinary clock face is always one button away.”

Avoid testimonials claiming the clock treated anxiety, changed medical outcomes, or accurately predicted a relative's life.

## Required prototype evidence

Kickstarter's [rules](https://www.kickstarter.com/rules) require honest presentation, and its [hardware guidance](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005134554-What-are-the-rules-for-hardware-and-product-design-projects) requires a working prototype rather than photorealistic renderings that could be mistaken for a finished product.

Before campaign submission, demonstrate:

### Hardware

- Display readable at normal desk distance
- Ten-or-more-digit total-seconds view or equivalent layout
- Physical mode controls
- Adjustable brightness and quiet-hour behavior
- USB-C power input and measured consumption
- Real-time clock and recovery after at least a one-hour power interruption
- Non-volatile configuration
- Factory reset
- Production-representative radio module if wireless setup is promised

### Firmware

- Current time and timezone
- Correct leap-year and daylight-saving behavior
- Total seconds lived
- Goal countdown and count-up
- Enabled estimate modes with persistent `EST` indicator
- Range/central-quantile provenance retained
- Corrupted or missing configuration fallback
- No negative countdown after an invalid date
- Signed update path or a disclosed update plan

### Companion

- Adults-only and goal-only routes
- Full questionnaire branching
- Range-first result and comprehension check
- Local calculation with visible source/model version
- Preview and transfer to a real clock
- Review, export, and delete
- Annual refresh flow or an honest disclosure that it is not yet developed
- No required account

### Continuous demo shot

Record one minimally edited shot that:

1. Identifies the prototype revision.
2. Powers it on.
3. Pairs or enters setup.
4. Sends a real goal and enabled modes.
5. Cycles through the resulting displays.
6. Disconnects network access and shows continued operation.
7. Power-cycles the clock and shows recovery.
8. Deletes the optional horizon and retains goal mode.

Do not composite numbers onto the screen, speed up setup without disclosure, or use a mobile mockup as if it is connected.

### Fixed campaign and production cost

Include:

- Electrical, mechanical, firmware, and companion development
- Industrial design and design-for-manufacture
- Tooling and fixtures

- Pilot build
- Photography/video and campaign production
- Legal, privacy, trademark, and scientific review
- Kickstarter and payment fees, using current quoted rates
- Tax/accounting setup
- Minimum order commitments
- Schedule and cost contingency

### Price formula

Use a reviewed model such as:

`pledge price = landed variable cost / (1 - platform/payment rate - warranty/contingency rate - required gross margin)`

Then test pessimistic scenarios for:

- 15–25% component increase
- Lower assembly yield
- Freight increase
- Currency movement
- Certification re-test
- 5–10% replacement rate
- Late pledges and failed payments

The funding goal must cover fixed launch costs plus the minimum viable batch and contingency after fees and taxes. Shipping should be charged using destination estimates close to fulfillment unless the campaign's supported flow and backer expectations require another approach.

## Risk register

### Scientific credibility

Risk: No validated model supports a global 18+ lifetime estimate from the selected questions.  
Impact: Misleading claims, reputational harm, campaign rejection, or regulatory scrutiny.  
Mitigation: Ship the population life-table range as the scientific minimum; use survey personalization only inside validated boundaries; obtain independent review; publish model limitations.

### Emotional harm

Risk: A mortality display increases anxiety or is used coercively.  
Impact: Harm to users and trust.  
Mitigation: Adults-only, explicit opt-in, comfort and comprehension checks, range first, goal-only alternative, instant hide/delete, neutral gift setup, no alarming notifications, and user testing focused on discomfort.

### Privacy and security

Risk: Health answers, birth date, or inferred horizon leak from a phone, cloud service, logs, or paired clock.  
Impact: Sensitive-data exposure and legal obligations.  
Mitigation: Local-first architecture, no account, encrypted storage, no raw answers on the clock, authenticated pairing, no sensitive analytics, threat modelling, penetration testing, and tested deletion.

### Display supply and custom tooling

Risk: A chosen display has a high minimum order, long lead time, poor lifetime, or cannot show required formats.  
Impact: Cost increase or redesign.  
Mitigation: Qualify two options early; prototype with production-intent parts; negotiate allocation; avoid custom glass unless budget and schedule support it.

### Timekeeping accuracy

Risk: Drift, timezone bugs, daylight-saving changes, or power loss make a clock visibly wrong.  
Impact: Core product failure.  
Mitigation: Reviewed RTC design, backup power, timezone library/version strategy, firmware tests, long-duration drift tests, and manual correction.

### Companion compatibility

Risk: Web Bluetooth or mobile platform support excludes backers.  
Impact: Failed setup and support burden.  
Mitigation: Decide supported phones/browsers before launch, demonstrate each one, provide manual ordinary/goal setup, and state requirements prominently.

### Certification

Risk: Radio, EMC, electrical, materials, accessibility, recycling, or labelling tests fail or vary by market.  
Impact: Delay and unplanned cost.  
Mitigation: Select launch destinations deliberately, use pre-certified modules where useful, consult labs before layout freeze, run pre-scan tests, and budget a re-test.

### Power accessories

Risk: Region-specific adapters multiply inventory and compliance complexity.  
Impact: Shipping errors and cost.  
Mitigation: Prefer standard USB-C input and be explicit about whether a certified adapter or only a cable is included per market.

### Enclosure quality

Risk: Prototype finish does not translate to production, or assembly time is too high.  
Impact: Cosmetic complaints, scrap, and margin loss.  
Mitigation: Production-intent samples, tolerance analysis, finish testing, drop tests, assembly-time measurement, and approved golden samples.

### Cost and logistics

Risk: Component, freight, duty, tax, currency, replacement, or fulfillment costs exceed assumptions.  
Impact: Underfunding.  
Mitigation: Destination-specific quotes, conservative quantities, contingency, realistic gross margin, limited variants, and scenario analysis.

### Firmware maintenance

Risk: Bugs or security vulnerabilities remain after shipment.  
Impact: Incorrect displays or compromised devices.  
Mitigation: Signed updates, supported update path, model/source versioning, vulnerability contact, maintenance budget, and ordinary offline fallback.

### Scope growth

Risk: Wearables, cloud sync, custom colours, or campaign stretch goals delay the core clock.  
Impact: Missed delivery and diluted quality.  
Mitigation: Freeze the reward specification; keep wearables and proprietary sensors outside first-shipment commitments.

## User-research plan

### Round 1 — Concept and language

Participants: 15 adults across ages, cultures, and attitudes toward mortality.  
Method: Moderated 30-minute interviews with low-fidelity concepts.  
Questions:

- Which modes have lasting value after the novelty fades?
- Does “adult motivational desk clock” match the product?
- What does “illustrative planning horizon” mean to the participant?
- Which wording feels motivating, manipulative, or frightening?
- Would the participant keep it visible at home or work?
- What would they expect to pay before seeing a suggested price?

Gate: At least 80% explain the core product without calling the output a known death date.

### Round 2 — Questionnaire

Participants: At least 15 cognitive interviews plus 50 unmoderated completions.  
Measure:

- Median and 90th-percentile completion time
- Drop-off by section
- Misunderstood response options
- Prefer-not-to-answer and missingness rates
- Ability to explain why country, birth date, and life-table category are requested
- Comfort before and after seeing a result
- Comprehension-check pass rate

Gate: Median completion at or below 10 minutes, no high-severity wording issue, and at least 80% understand uncertainty after one explanation.

### Round 3 — Prototype usability

Participants: 20 adults; include users with low vision, limited dexterity, and limited technical confidence.  
Tasks:

- Power on and configure ordinary time
- Add a goal
- Complete or decline the estimate
- Change default mode and brightness
- Find the range/source
- Hide and delete the horizon
- Recover after power interruption
- Factory-reset before gifting/resale

Gate: At least 80% change modes and delete the horizon without assistance; all participants can reach a neutral clock face quickly.

### Round 4 — Home beta

Participants: 30 adults for 2–4 weeks with production-intent prototypes.  
Measure:

- Daily/weekly continued use by mode, with participant diaries rather than invasive telemetry
- Display readability and brightness
- Clock drift and power recovery
- Pairing failures and support burden
- Whether motivation persists after novelty
- Anxiety, avoidance, sleep disruption, coercion, or household conflict
- Hardware heat, noise, reset, cable, and enclosure issues
- Return intent and acceptable price

Stop or redesign if a repeated serious distress pattern appears. Do not average severe reports away with a high satisfaction score.

### Willingness-to-pay method

Ask open-ended expected price before showing any proposed price. Then test a small set of randomized price points and purchase-intent questions. Compare claimed intent with refundable reservation or email-confirmation behaviour only if legally and ethically reviewed. Do not treat social-media likes as demand.

## Campaign FAQ

### Does this clock predict when I will die?

No. No questionnaire can know an individual's death date. The optional feature shows a broad statistical planning range and lets an adult choose one illustrative point to display. Actual outcomes can be very different.

### Is it a medical device or medical advice?

It is intended as a motivational general-wellness desk clock, not for diagnosis, treatment, emergency decisions, financial planning, or end-of-life decisions. The final intended-use and marketing language will be reviewed for each launch market.

### Is the health questionnaire required?

No. Time lived, goals, milestones, habits, and ordinary time work without health questions.

### Who can use the estimated mode?

Adults 18+ who choose it and fall within the supported data/model boundaries. Goal-only modes remain available to everyone permitted by the product's general age policy.

### Why do you ask for country/region and a life-table category?

Public life tables differ by location, age, and commonly published sex categories. These fields select a population baseline. A blended or regional fallback is available when a category is declined or unavailable, with reduced specificity disclosed.

### Do all questionnaire answers change the range?

No. Only factors supported by the named model can change it. Other answers are context or preferences and are labelled as such. Until a suitable personalized model is approved, the result uses the population baseline only.

### Where are my answers stored?

In the planned MVP, health answers and calculations stay in encrypted local storage on the companion device by default. The clock receives only the minimum display configuration. No account or cloud health upload is required.

### Can I delete the estimate and keep the clock?

Yes. You can delete health answers and result history while keeping ordinary time, goals, and habits. A factory reset clears personal configuration from the clock.

### Does it need the internet?

After time and modes are configured, the clock is designed to work offline. The final campaign will state any internet requirement for setup, timezone updates, firmware updates, or optional features.

### What happens every year?

If you opt into one annual reminder, the companion asks you to review changed answers. It shows the old and new range and does not update the physical horizon until you confirm. No reminder is sent if you decline.

### Will wearable integration be included?

Not in the first-shipment promise unless it is fully implemented, validated, and demonstrated before launch. Read-only HealthKit and Health Connect support is a future research direction, not a reason to back the current reward.

### Are you building your own wearable?

Not for this campaign. A proprietary wearable would require a separate sensor, manufacturing, validation, support, privacy, and regulatory program.

### Is there a subscription?

The core clock and local setup should not require one. If optional services are developed later, they cannot remove purchased offline functionality.

### Can I give it as a gift?

Yes, but it arrives in neutral clock mode. The recipient privately chooses whether to enable any estimate; the buyer cannot answer health questions for them.

### What if the display makes me uncomfortable?

Switch to current time or a goal with one action, hide lifespan modes, or delete the profile. The estimated mode is optional and should not be used if it causes distress.

### Is it battery powered?

The MVP is planned for USB-C wired power so an always-on display can update each second reliably. Any battery or backup-duration claim will be based on measured production-intent hardware.

### Which phones and countries are supported?

These will be listed before pledges open, after companion compatibility, life-table licensing, compliance, power accessories, shipping, taxes, and returns are validated. Do not pledge based on an unlisted destination or phone.

### What does Kickstarter fund?

Final engineering, tooling/production setup, compliance testing, pilot production, manufacturing, packaging, fulfillment, and support for the demonstrated product. The campaign page will separate completed work from remaining work.

## Claim-review checklist

Before any claim appears in a page, video, ad, influencer brief, interview, packaging, or app listing, record:

- Exact claim
- Intended audience and market
- Evidence source and version
- Whether the working prototype demonstrates it
- Required qualifier displayed with equal prominence
- Scientific reviewer approval
- Legal/privacy reviewer approval
- Expiration/review date

Automatically reject claims containing:

- Exact, accurate, precise, proven, clinical, diagnosis, prevention, treatment, or “adds years” without approved evidence and context
- Implied certainty from a seconds-level display
- A medical outcome testimonial
- Fear-based urgency
- A feature included only on the roadmap

## Recommended campaign decision

Proceed toward a prototype and user validation because the physical, glanceable time concept is distinctive enough to test. Keep the first campaign viable even if scientific review rejects survey-based personalization: the population range, time-lived display, goals, habits, and finite-moment modes still form a complete product.

The campaign should not launch on the promise that a ten-minute survey predicts lifespan closely. Credibility, user control, and a real working prototype are the product's long-term advantage—not false precision.
